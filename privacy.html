Contact for anything on this page, including a request about your data: support@velvetviking.co.uk. Everything below describes real, current behaviour, verified against the code.

Valhalla is a running-training app. Most of what it does happens entirely on your own device. This page explains exactly what leaves your device, when, and why — no more and no less than that.

The short version

  • By default, your training plan lives only on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • If you turn on sign-in to back up your plan or use it on more than one device, your email address and training plan are stored in your account with our database provider, and nowhere else.
  • Strava sync is switched off for this private beta, so no Strava data is read or stored for any beta tester. When it is available, it reads your recent running activities to help log your training — it never writes anything back to Strava, and never sees your Strava password.
  • We don't run ads, don't sell data, don't use tracking/analytics scripts, and no AI or language model service ever receives your training data. Valhalla's coaching logic is deterministic code, not an AI model, and calls no AI service itself.
  • You can delete your account and everything we hold on our servers at any time from Settings, or by asking us. The copy on your own phone or browser is removed by you.

What's stored on your device only

By default — with no account and no sign-in — everything Valhalla knows about your training is kept in your browser's local storage on your own device: your plan, your logged workouts, your notes, your preferences. This never leaves your device unless you explicitly export a backup file yourself.

What's stored if you turn on sign-in

Sign-in is optional and off by default. If you choose to sign in, you get a link by email, so there is no password for you to create or remember. The following is then stored with our database provider, Supabase:

DataWhy
Your email addressTo send you sign-in links and identify your account
Your training plan and log, as one recordSo it backs up and follows you to another device

This data is protected so that only your own signed-in session can read it — not other users, and not us browsing the database casually. Supabase hosts this data on our behalf as our data processor; Vercel hosts the app itself and the small server functions that talk to Supabase and Strava on your behalf, so tokens and credentials never reach your device.

What's stored if you connect Strava

Not available during the private beta. Strava sync is switched off for this beta while we confirm approval with Strava, so no Strava data is read, received or stored for any beta tester. The Connect control is not offered, and the server refuses a connection even if the app is bypassed. This section describes how it works when it is switched back on.

Connecting Strava is optional. If you connect it:

  • Your Strava sign-in tokens are stored server-side only, protected so that no browser — including your own — can read them back out. They exist purely so the server can fetch your activities on your behalf.
  • From each running activity, we read and store: distance, duration, pace, heart rate, cadence, and elevation gain. We do not store GPS routes or maps, photos, or any Strava social data (kudos, comments, followers).
  • The connection is read-only — Valhalla cannot post, edit, or delete anything in your Strava account, and never requests permission to.
  • You can disconnect Strava at any time from Settings. Disconnecting stops any further access and deletes the copy of your Strava activity data we hold server-side — it isn't kept around after you disconnect. Values already merged into your own training log (distance, pace, heart rate and so on, logged against a specific day in your plan) are your record of your own run and stay in your log, the same as anything you type in yourself — disconnecting Strava doesn't reach back and strip them out.

How long we keep it, and how to delete it

On our servers. If you signed in, your account and stored plan are kept until the account is deleted. You can do that yourself at any time from Settings, and it removes your email, your stored plan, your Strava connection and any staged Strava activity data in one action. If you are a beta tester and would rather just ask, email support@velvetviking.co.uk and we will delete your account and all identifiable personal and training data Velvet Viking holds on its servers within 30 days of receiving your request.

On your own device. This part we cannot reach, and we would rather say so than imply otherwise. Your plan, your logged runs and your saved sign-in are stored by the app on your own phone or browser. Deleting your account does not erase them, and neither does anything we can do from our side. They stay under your control: Reset Plan in Settings clears the training plan itself, and clearing the app's or browser's stored data, or uninstalling the app, removes everything the app has kept locally — including any plan parked on a shared device and your stored sign-in.

If your phone backs its apps up to your own cloud account — Android's own backup, for example — a copy may exist there too. That backup belongs to you and your device maker, not to Velvet Viking, and we have no way to reach into it. Your device's backup settings are where that is managed.

Your rights

  • Access — export a full copy of your data at any time from Settings.
  • Correction — edit your plan, logs, and settings directly in the app.
  • Deletion — delete your account and everything stored on our servers, from Settings, or by asking us at support@velvetviking.co.uk. Copies on your own device are removed by you, as described above.

This section describes the mechanisms actually built into the app. It isn't a claim that this service meets every requirement of GDPR, CCPA, or any other specific privacy law in full — if that assurance matters for your audience or jurisdiction, get that confirmed by someone qualified to say so.
