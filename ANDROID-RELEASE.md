# Android — Google Play submission readiness

`ANDROID-APP-LINKS.md` covers the signing key and App Link verification. This
covers everything else Play asks for.

The app is a **Capacitor WebView** pointed at `https://app.velvetviking.co.uk`.
That single fact answers most of the store questions below, and it is why the
release is small: there is almost no native surface.

### The microphone permission, and what to declare for it

`RECORD_AUDIO` is requested **at runtime**, at the moment the athlete presses the
Ask Coach microphone, and by nothing else in the app. Valhalla never opens an
audio stream itself: Android's recogniser consumes the audio and returns a
transcript string, and the plugin's audio-buffer callback is deliberately empty.

**Only the on-device recogniser is used.** Android's ordinary `SpeechRecognizer`
may send audio to the device's speech service provider; Valhalla asks for
`createOnDeviceSpeechRecognizer()` and refuses rather than fall back to the
networked one, so a phone without on-device recognition simply types instead.
That is what makes the Data Safety answer straightforward:

- audio is **not collected** and **not shared** — it never leaves the device and
  Valhalla never receives it;
- the resulting **transcript** is the athlete's question and is sent to
  Valhalla's server and on to the model as text, not stored, and not logged.

`<queries>` for `android.speech.RecognitionService` is required from Android 11:
without it the recogniser is invisible to package-visibility filtering and every
device reports "no speech recognition". `android.hardware.microphone` is
declared `required="false"` so a device without one can still install and use
Valhalla, where Ask Coach accepts a typed question exactly as before.

The full stage-by-stage flow is in `VOICE-DATA-FLOW.md`.

`android/app/src/main/assets/capacitor.config.json` is **generated by
`cap sync`** and is gitignored. Run `cap sync android` before building or the
built APK carries whatever host was last synced.

## Deep links

Two mechanisms, on purpose:

- **App Links** (`autoVerify="true"`) on `app.velvetviking.co.uk/auth`,
  `/start`, and the legacy `*.vercel.app/auth`. These only work once an
  installed build's signing certificate matches the fingerprint published in
  `/.well-known/assetlinks.json`.
- **Custom scheme** `com.velvetviking.valhalla://auth` — needs no verification
  and no stable key, and is what carries the magic-link tokens into the app
  today.

The runtime picks the custom scheme when it is inside the shell, so sign-in
works on a debug build before App Links ever verify.

## Payments — the posture, and why it is compliant

**Nothing is sold inside the app.** Google Play's Payments policy requires Play
Billing for digital content purchased *in* the app; it does not require it for a
subscription bought on the open web and then used by signing in.

Both purchase surfaces are suppressed inside the native shell:

- the account/settings controls — `renderSubscriptionActions()` shows a sentence
  about where the subscription is managed, never a subscribe or portal link;
- **`/start`, the page with the prices on it** — the interval choice, the prices
  and the trial button are replaced with a sentence. The manifest routes
  `/start` links into the app, so this page was reachable inside the shell and
  is the one that mattered.

Hiding only the button would leave the prices on screen, which is still the
steering the rule forbids: the rule is about *presenting* the external purchase,
not about the tap that completes it.

If Play Billing is ever wanted, the `google` provider rail already exists in the
canonical model — see `STORE-COMMERCIAL-BOUNDARY.md`. It is an adapter, not a
migration.

| Health & fitness — *fitness info* (distance, pace, duration, workouts) | yes | no | App functionality | no |
| Health & fitness — *health info* (heart rate, readiness, how a session felt) | yes | no | App functionality, personalisation | **yes** |
| Purchase history | yes | no | App functionality | no |
| App interactions | **no** | — | — | — |
| Location | **no** | — | — | — |
| Device or other IDs | **no** | — | — | — |

Also declare:

- **Data is encrypted in transit** — yes.
- **Users can request data deletion** — yes, and the route is **in-app**:
  Settings → Delete my account. Also `support@velvetviking.co.uk`.
- **Users can request their data** — yes, in-app export.
- **Committed to the Play Families policy** — not applicable; not aimed at
  children.

Two of those answers are the ones a reviewer checks against the app: the
optional-health-data flag is true because the health-and-readiness consent is
genuinely optional and default off, and the deletion route is in-app because it
is, not merely by email.

## Account deletion URL

Play requires a **web URL** for deletion requests even when the route is in-app.
Use `https://velvetviking.co.uk/delete-account` — Website must publish a page
there explaining the in-app route and the email fallback, and stating what is
deleted and what is retained. `LEGAL-FACTS.md` §6 has the exact facts.

## Assets required

- App icon 512×512 PNG (32-bit, alpha)
- Feature graphic 1024×500 PNG or JPEG
- **Phone screenshots:** minimum 2, maximum 8. 16:9 or 9:16, each side between
  320px and 3840px
- 7-inch and 10-inch tablet screenshots if tablet support is declared
- Screenshots must be of the real app. A screenshot showing a price is a
  screenshot Play may read as an in-app purchase — prefer the plan, the day
  card and the coaching surfaces.

## Testing track

Go **closed testing** first, not production:

1. Internal testing (up to 100 testers, instant) — confirm install, App Links,
   sign-in and the WebView load on a real device.
2. Closed testing — the beta cohort.
3. Production only when the commercial switches are on and legal has approved.

Google requires a period of closed testing with a minimum number of testers
before a **personal** developer account may publish to production. A company
account does not have that requirement. Check which type the account is before
planning the timeline — it is the single most common cause of a delayed launch.

## Tester accounts

Reviewers need to get in, and **public signup is closed**. Supply Play with a
demo account whose address is on the beta allowlist, in the review notes, with
the note that sign-in is by emailed magic link and no password exists. Without
that, review fails at the first screen.
